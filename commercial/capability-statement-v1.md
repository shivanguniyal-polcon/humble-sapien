## What we do

We build analytical instruments that answer one question about one policy, publish
them free for citizens and small organisations, and licence them to the institutions
that can pay. The code a ministry runs is the code a village worker runs. That is the
product and the constraint.

Nine instruments run today. Three carry client work:

- **The Analysis Battery.** Eleven modules — cost-benefit, cost-effectiveness,
  distributional CBA, Markov cohort, budget impact, multi-criteria ranking, scenario
  runs against a calibrated PM-JAY reference model, stakeholder games, queueing,
  Monte Carlo. Every run emits an audit manifest: SHA-256 hashes over input,
  parameters and output, plus the engine version.
- **The Evaluation Engine.** OLS, logit, 2SLS, difference-in-differences with event
  studies, sharp and fuzzy RDD, synthetic control, propensity score matching. Runs in
  the browser on built-in Census and NFHS coverage, or on your files.
- **The Policy Simplifier.** Plain-language rewriting of statutes and notifications
  with provenance, risk flags, confidence scoring and five jurisdiction dictionaries
  (India, UK, US, EU, Singapore).

Underneath them sit two datasets the public can take apart: a Data Commons with a
contract per dataset (tier, rows, unit, provenance), and a Regulator Obligation
Monitor that reconstructs what Indian financial regulators actually require, with
every extracted obligation quoted back to its source sentence.

## How we work

1. **The question first.** A deliverable starts with the client's decision, not our
   methods list. If the design cannot answer the question on the data available, we
   say so before the engagement starts.
2. **Receipts on every number.** Absence receipts for missing data, audit manifests
   for runs, source spans for rewrites. Nothing is imputed quietly.
3. **Refusal over decoration.** Where a design fails its own requirements — too few
   units, no pre-period, no overlap at the cutoff — the tool refuses and states what
   it would need, rather than printing a number nobody can interpret.
4. **Published in the open.** Commissioned modules enter the public engine by
   default, with the commissioning terms published. Method independence is
   contractual.

## Selected evidence

- **PM-JAY fiscal sustainability model.** System-dynamics projection of India's
  largest publicly funded health assurance scheme to 2035. Calibrated against 28
  official anchors (PIB releases, Lok Sabha replies, NHA Annual Report 2024-25):
  cards 4.5% MAPE, admissions 4.5%, empanelled hospitals 2.8%, treatment value 4.3%.
  Four scenarios under one budget envelope; no tested lever restores fiscal
  sustainability.
- **Regulator Obligation Monitor.** 50 RBI circulars, 2,903 trigger-matched rows,
  typed with a seven-way obligation classifier. Four blind rounds: 92.1% strict duty
  precision, 98.0% type accuracy, 100% duty recall on the final holdout. The
  applicability filter dropped zero binding obligations across two buyer profiles in
  110 hand-judged drops.
- **Policy simplifier readability.** The built-in worked example moves from Flesch
  18.9 (graduate level) to 64.7 (8th–9th grade) with every rewrite tied to the exact
  source characters it came from.
- **Field record.** 60 parliamentary questions drafted for a Member of the 17th Lok
  Sabha; scheme delivery monitored across five states; the atlas covers 90 flagship
  policies across 15 sectors.

licences USD 12,000–25,000 a year; Commons Plus USD 2,000–6,000 a seat; support
agreements USD 10,000–40,000 banded by training days at USD 400–600 a day; commissioned
modules USD 25,000–75,000. Individuals and small civil society organisations pay
nothing, permanently, under the mission lock.
