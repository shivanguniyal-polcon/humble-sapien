# Concept note: DPI corridor landscape — UPI, Pix, NIP

**For:** a funder, regulator or operator comparing digital public infrastructure
across South Asia, Latin America and Africa · **From:** The Humble Sapien LLP ·
22 October 2026 · valid 60 days

---

## The problem

"Learn from India's UPI" is repeated in ministerial delegations and donor decks with
almost no structural comparison behind it. UPI, Brazil's Pix and Nigeria's NIP are
different designs solving different problems: a rail layered on bank accounts, a
central-bank rail built to displace cash, a switch operated by the banking pool. The
design choices — account model, pricing, operator governance, offline behaviour —
are what travel across borders. Volume leaderboards are not.

## Objective

A sourced, comparable read of three DPI corridors: what each one is, how it is
governed and priced, who it reaches and who it misses, and which design choices a
new adopter can actually copy given its own account infrastructure.

## Scope

Three corridors, one region each:

| Corridor | Operator | Region |
|---|---|---|
| UPI (India) | NPCI | South Asia |
| Pix (Brazil) | BCB | Latin America |
| NIP (Nigeria) | NIBSS | Africa |

Per corridor: launch and adoption trajectory, transaction volume and value with
source and as-of date, governance and operator model, pricing model, interoperability
and offline behaviour, inclusion read (who uses it, who is left out), and the
regulatory moves that shaped it.

## Method

1. **Primary sources only for the numbers.** NPCI statistics, BCB Pix documentation
   and annual reports, NIBSS reports. Every figure carries its source and its as-of
   date. Where two primary sources disagree, both are printed.
2. **Design comparison on a fixed frame.** Each corridor is scored against the same
   frame — account model, rail type, operator, pricing, offline, inclusion — so the
   comparison is structural, not narrative.
3. **Name the gaps.** Where a number is not published (pricing terms are the usual
   one), the page says so rather than estimating. A named gap survives review; a
   guessed number does not.
4. **THS's own instrument as evidence.** The India side is grounded in the
   organisation's field record: a UPI-as-DPI parliamentary question drafted for the
   17th Lok Sabha, and the ATLAS comparison method already used across 90 policy
   instruments in 15 sectors.

## What you get

- A landscape page: the three corridors on the fixed frame, every figure cited.
- A design-decision memo: which choices are transferable, under which conditions,
  and the two or three that look copyable but are not.
- The source table behind it, machine-readable, so your team can re-run the
  comparison when the numbers move.

## What it is not

It is not a market-share study. Commercial data on payment volumes by private
operator is paywalled, so this is a regulatory and structural landscape with named
data gaps, not a competitive map.
