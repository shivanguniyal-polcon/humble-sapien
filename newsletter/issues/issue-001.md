## Lead

By March 2026, 314 central schemes — 54 centrally sponsored and 260 central sector — are due for re-appraisal, according to a PIB note from May 2025. It is the kind of line that scrolls past in a press release and nobody stops over. We think it is worth stopping over, because a re-appraisal is the moment a government asks whether a scheme worked, and the answer depends entirely on whether anyone outside the government can read the scheme in the first place.

On the sample statutory text we scored, the Flesch Reading Ease came out at 18.9 — graduate level. Rewritten in near-casual language, the same clause scored 63.7, standard eighth-to-ninth grade. Nothing was removed. Only the phrasing moved.

The re-appraisal deadline is a useful lens on the wider problem. About 890 Central Acts are in force, by a reported count from May 2025, and nobody holds the real total of state laws, rules and notifications. The national open data platform passed five lakh resources in August 2024. In 2023-24, 83 Central Acts were authenticated in regional languages. The supply of rule is enormous and growing; the supply of *readable* rule is close to fixed. Every year the gap widens, and power quietly transfers to the people who can parse the unreadable version — lawyers, consultants, officers, the already-connected — while everyone else pays in time, travel, and forfeited entitlements they never knew they had.

We think this is fixable, and not only with software. It is a dignity problem before it is a design problem. When a scheme is written in language only an officer can parse, the citizen is not a citizen. She is a supplicant.

## Field note

We are careful with language here, and it is worth saying why. "No public evidence found" is not the same claim as "it did not happen." The first is a statement about receipts: the promise was made in public, and the paper that would prove delivery was not left where anyone can read it. The second is a claim about reality, which we usually cannot make.

That distinction runs through every scorecard, brief and dataset we publish. It is the difference between accountability and accusation, and it is why the strictest status on our promise scorecards still reads, honestly, as a question rather than a verdict. If the record is missing, the ask is simple: leave paper.
